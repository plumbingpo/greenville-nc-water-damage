Roof Leak Water Damage Help in Greenville

The spot on your ceiling is seldom directly below the hole in the roof. Rain slips under a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a tired skylight seal, then runs along the decking and rafters before it drops onto the insulation. By the time a brown ring shows on the ceiling, the attic above it has often been wet for a while. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

Our trade is water damage restoration, and roofing is a separate one. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. When the roof is still letting water through, a temporary tarp is something we can install, and we will coordinate with your roofer so the repair comes before the ceiling is closed.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsTurn off the breaker for any light, fan or outlet that has water running through or near it, then keep your hands off it.
  2. Catch the DripsFurniture, electronics and rugs should be moved aside, with buckets placed wherever water is falling. Plastic or towels spread on the floor will keep it from soaking.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeWhen water pools in a bubble overhead, pierce the low point with a screwdriver while standing clear, and let it run into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Photograph the DamageBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Interior damage from a sudden, accidental roof leak, like one caused by a fallen limb or wind-torn shingles, is often covered under a homeowners policy. A leak put down to an old, worn or neglected roof is usually denied, and fixing the roof may be treated separately from the interior work. We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your adjuster, but coverage depends on your policy, so read it or ask your agent.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Flashing that has rusted, lifted or pulled away around chimneys, vents and walls.
  • Skylight seals and curbs that dry out, crack and let rain run inside.
  • Wind-torn, curling or split shingles that leave the underlayment open to rain.
  • Popped or exposed roofing nails, each one a tiny opening for rain.
  • Gutters packed with leaves, which push water back beneath the first row of shingles.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Hidden Attic Mold

Damp framing, paper-faced drywall, warmth and darkness give mold what it needs. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours and spread across the underside of the decking.

Drywall Ceilings

Drywall that stays wet stains, sags and loses its strength. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Insulation in the Attic

Once soaked, insulation flattens, loses its value and keeps the drywall under it damp for days. Drying is sometimes possible with fiberglass. Cellulose that is saturated generally comes out.

Lights and Wiring

Recessed cans, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are low points where roof water collects and drips through. Keep that circuit off until someone has inspected it, since damp wiring may corrode or short out.
